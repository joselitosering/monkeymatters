Market Temperament
A DOVISH JOBS PRINT AND AN OIL RELEASE ARE DOING THE WORK — THE LONG BOND STILL WON'T BLINK Friday's September jobs report was a genuine miss, not a rounding error: +29,000 payrolls against a ~84,000 consensus, -60,000 in downward revisions to the prior two months, unemployment up to 4.2%, and wage growth cooling to 3.0% YoY (Bloomberg/CNBC). Markets read it exactly as a cooling-labor-market, lower-hike-odds print should be read — Kalshi's implied probability of an October 28 Fed hike (off September's 3.75%-4.00% hike, the first since 2023) fell to roughly 21%, and equities rallied hard into the close: S&P 500 +0.73%, Nasdaq +1.19%, Dow +0.49%. The nuance worth getting right, and the one this pipeline keeps flagging: the 10-Year Treasury yield did NOT cooperate. It closed Friday at 5.28%, up 4 basis points on the day the market was supposedly pricing in a dovish Fed — still a 2002-era high, and a reminder that persistent inflation, heavy fiscal borrowing and AI-infrastructure capex demand for capital are structural forces a single jobs miss doesn't fix. Overnight brought a second major catalyst: the G7 agreed to release 100 million barrels of crude and diesel from emergency reserves, reportedly after pressure from President Trump, while OPEC+ held its November production targets unchanged — both read as supply-relief moves that are pushing WTI down toward $89 (a second straight down day). Brent is holding firmer, above $102, which is the more honest read on geopolitical risk: Saudi-backed forces have launched an offensive against Houthi-held territory near the Bab el-Mandeb strait, a live escalation of the broader conflict corridor this pipeline has tracked since the Strait of Hormuz campaign began — yet regional crude exports are reportedly running at a record 22.5 million barrels per day. Read together: the market is being handed two separate pieces of good news (softer labor data, an oil-supply release) on the same morning that the two things actually driving the structural risk premium — long-end yields and live Middle East conflict — haven't resolved. VIX's +5% premarket pop to 16.10, while still a low absolute level, is a tell that the options market isn't fully buying the all-clear either, with Wednesday's FOMC Minutes (2:00 PM ET, from the September hike meeting) as the week's real event risk: watch specifically for language on oil pass-through to inflation and whether officials still see a second 2026 hike as likely after Friday's data. Our own futures tape reflects the same two-sidedness: /ES at 7,775.50 is barely changed, sitting just above both the classic pivot (7,770.00) and Friday's POC (7,774.00), after an overnight high (7,793.00) that poked 2 points above Friday's VAH (7,791.00) before fading back inside — an unconfirmed, shallow probe. /NQ is the more interesting story: it spiked to an overnight high of 31,221.25, clearing Friday's VAH (31,170.00) and closing in on the classic R1 (31,300.92) and the Donchian-20 high (31,282.50), before giving back nearly 200 points to the current 31,022.75 — now sitting BELOW its own pivot (31,030.58) and barely above Friday's VAL (31,015.00), a materially weaker setup than Friday's own +1.19% Nasdaq close would suggest. A broad Brazilian-equities basket (XP, STNE, ITUB, BBD, EWZ and leveraged ETFs like BRZU) rallied double digits Friday on election results — a regional, not a US-macro, story.
