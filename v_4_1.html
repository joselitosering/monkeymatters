Abstract. This white paper presents a practical, legally compliant, multi‑horizon program that fuses the classical Turtle framework (Donchian breakouts with ATR‑based risk) with regime detection and options overlays. We show how to operate across futures, FX, and equities, scale intraday execution from daily context, and incorporate tax‑aware structuring without diluting the system’s core statistical edge. 0) Quick Start — ES Intraday Turtle

Pull the daily Donchian rails (H20/L20; H55/L55) and Nᵈ = ATR(20). Drop to 5–30m and compute Nⁱ. Accept a breakout only on a 5m close beyond the rail (or body ≥ ~0.2×Nⁱ). Stop=2×Nⁱ; Adds=0.5×Nⁱ steps; Exit=L10ⁱ/H10ⁱ (S1) or L20ⁱ/H20ⁱ (S2). Flat by EOD unless risk is handed to a defined‑risk option.

Example: H20=5530; Nᵈ≈75; Nⁱ=12.5 → Entry 5530.25; Stop 5505.25; Adds 5536.50/5542.75/5549.00; Equity 100k @ 0.5% → UnitRisk $500; N/ES $625 → 8 MES per unit.

1) Motivation & Executive Thesis

Most discretionary programs fail by wagering on direction while ignoring risk translation. Turtle Shell corrects that: it buys strength, sells weakness, and standardizes every decision in the volatility unit N = ATR(20). Small, bounded losses finance episodic large gains. The Shell—a separate options sleeve—adds income in balanced regimes and convexity in trends without capping the futures’ first unit. The result is a positively skewed return stream that remains robust across assets and market cycles.

Thesis. Daily rails for intent; 5–30‑minute execution for precision; options for regime‑aware carry/convexity; tax‑aware structure for capital efficiency. Keep rules mechanical and throttle only by regime.
Action items. (1) Fix default risk/unit at 0.25–0.50% (evaluation) then 0.5–1.0%. (2) Enforce daily stop 1.5–2.0R and a two‑strike rule. (3) Pyramid only after progress at +0.5N steps; never add to losers. (4) Maintain an audit log of rails, N, orders, fills, and rule adherence.

Supporting evidence from managed‑futures indices shows that programs which enforce volatility‑scaled losses and allow asymmetric gains have historically delivered positive skew and low correlation to equities during crisis windows. While edges compress in quiet regimes, disciplined risk translation into N maintains survival until the opportunity set expands.

Research note: volatility targeting (via N) reduces variance of drawdowns and keeps realized leverage stable; behavioral under‑reaction and mandate constraints provide the economic fuel for breakouts.

2) Evidence for Trend & Why It Persists

Time‑series momentum appears across commodities, rates, FX, and equities, with the strongest Sharpe at 6–12‑month horizons and defensive behavior in equity sell‑offs. Donchian‑style breakouts harvest this by forcing participation only when price escapes its recent bounds; risk measured in N keeps losses comparable across markets and regimes.

Persistence arises from under‑reaction, slow‑moving capital, mandates, and policy cycles; these create delayed diffusion of information. Volatility targeting stabilizes realized leverage so the same rules work in both quiet and stormy periods, reducing the urge to over‑fit.

Action items. (1) Track N percentiles and ADX slope as regime tells. (2) Pre‑declare “trend‑eligible” vs “range‑only” each morning. (3) Report weekly KPIs by regime: hit rate, avg win/loss (R), time‑in‑market, and Shell contribution.

Meta‑analyses comparing breakout and moving‑average rules find broadly similar performance once risk is expressed in common units; the heavy lifting comes from cutting losses quickly and letting trends breathe, not from a specific filter. Transaction‑cost‑adjusted tests remain positive across futures complexes with turnover similar to S1/S2.

Research note: diversification across asset classes and term structures (e.g., STIRs vs bonds) increases crisis‑hedge reliability as correlation regimes shift.

3) Core Methodology (Turtle S1/S2)

Signals. S1: enter on H20/L20 breakout; exit on L10/H10. S2: enter on H55/L55 breakout; exit on L20/H20. Compute channels on prior bars (offset=1) so orders are staged at known prices.

Risk unit N. N=ATR(20) (Wilder). $ per 1N/contract = N×PV×Mult. Contracts per unit = floor(UnitRisk$ / $ per 1N/contract). Portfolio heat ≤ 4–6%.

Stops & Adds. Initial stop = 2N. Adds at +0.5N steps (long) or −0.5N (short) up to four units; never add to losers. Skip the very next re‑entry after an S1 loss to reduce immediate whipsaw.

Action items. (1) Lock add spacing at 0.5N; (2) Do not trail stops discretionarily—exit only on L10/L20; (3) Use micros whenever $ per 1N/ES exceeds UnitRisk$.

Research note: Donchian rules pass robustness tests across lookbacks (10–60 bars) when combined with ATR‑based stops and fixed add spacing; the close‑beyond filter reduces microstructure whipsaws.

Example — /ES S1 Long (H20 Break)

Assume prior‑day H20 = 5530.00, daily Nᵈ ≈ 75, and on 5‑minute bars Nⁱ = 12.5. Place a stop‑buy at 5530.25 (+1 tick). On fill, initial stop = 5530.25 − 2×12.5 = 5505.25. Add levels at +0.5×Nⁱ: 5536.50, 5542.75, 5549.00. With $100k equity and 0.5% risk/unit, UnitRisk$ = $500. $ per 1N/ES = 12.5×$50 = $625 → trade MES: $ per 1N/MES = $62.50 → 8 MES per unit.

Example — /ES S2 Short (L55 Break)

Suppose L55 = 5450.00, Nⁱ = 14.0. Stop‑sell at 5449.75. Initial stop = 5449.75 + 2×14.0 = 5477.75. Adds at −0.5×Nⁱ: 5442.75, 5435.75, 5428.75. Size as above using $ per 1N = Nⁱ×$50 (ES) or ×$5 (MES).

4) Intraday From Daily Rails (5–30m Execution)

Overview. Use daily H20/L20 (H55/L55 for S2) and Nᵈ for context; execute on 5–30‑minute bars with Nⁱ=ATR(20,5m) for stops, adds, and unit size. Flat by EOD unless risk is consciously handed to an option.

Trigger. Arm a stop at the daily rail (+1 tick). Accept only if a 5‑minute bar closes beyond the line or shows ≥0.2×Nⁱ body past it; this filters wick‑throughs while preserving trend participation.

Sizing. $ per 1N/contract = Nⁱ×PV×Mult. Contracts/unit = floor(UnitRisk$ / $ per 1N/contract). Keep heat ≤ 4–6%.

Action items. (1) Compute Nᵈ on the close; Nⁱ intraday. (2) Stage adds at fill time; no manual “chasing”. (3) Time‑stop into the close if exit signal hasn’t printed.

Research note: requiring a bar close beyond a threshold improves post‑signal drift vs intrabar triggers; sizing by Nⁱ aligns stop distance with realized noise of the traded timeframe.

Concrete /ES Examples

Daily trigger: Prior‑day H20=5530.00. Arm stop‑buy 5530.25. Intraday confirm: take only if a 5m candle closes above 5530.00 or has ≥0.2×Nⁱ body beyond. Stops/Adds: Nⁱ=12.5 → stop 2×Nⁱ (−25.0) and adds every +6.25 points.

5) Regime Classification: Trending · Balanced · Stressed

Purpose. Regime tagging doesn’t change the Turtle math; it changes the throttle. We classify each session using price state (inside/outside Donchian rails), directional strength (ADX slope), realized volatility percentiles (Nⁱ), and a compact set of market internals: VIX, TRIN (Arms Index), NYSE TICK, breadth, and opening gap size. Levels are indicative; use z‑scores vs a rolling window to adapt thresholds by product.

Interpretation. Trending days feature price escaping the rails with rising ADX and internals aligned (e.g., TRIN persistently < 0.8 for uptrends or > 1.2 for downtrends; TICK clustering on the dominant side). Balanced days show price inside rails, ADX flat, and internals oscillating around neutral (TRIN ~0.9–1.2; TICK mean near 0 with shallow tails). Stressed days combine top‑decile Nⁱ, outsized gaps, and disorderly internals (VIX spike > ~24, repeated ±1000 TICK prints, TRIN > 1.5).

Action items. (1) Morning dashboard: ADX slope (5m), Nⁱ percentile, VIX level & z‑score, TRIN (open + 60‑min avg), cumulative TICK slope. (2) If conflict, default to Balanced until the rail resolves. (3) Freeze regime for 30–60 minutes after tagging to avoid thrash.

Research note: internals are context, not triggers; they reduce false positives by requiring breadth/volume participation and aligned risk appetite.

6) Model Suite per Regime (with Scalping Protocols)

Trending (S1 default; S2 when stressed). Trade with the daily break; confirm with a 5‑minute close beyond H20/L20 (or H55/L55 when stressed). Initial stop = 2×Nⁱ; adds at ±0.5×Nⁱ up to four units as price makes you right. Exit on L10ⁱ/H10ⁱ (S1) or L20ⁱ/H20ⁱ (S2). Avoid counter‑trend scalps; internals should rhyme with direction.

Balanced (range). Inside H20/L20 with muted ADX and neutral internals: small, symmetric mean‑reversion around VWAP. Stops 1.0×Nⁱ; targets 1.0–1.5×Nⁱ. Two‑strike rule per side; abandon immediately on expansion or rail break. Shell (Sec. 7) may run income (condors/flies) around the rails.

Stressed (volatile). Widen buffers (+2–3 ticks), halve per‑unit risk, prefer micros, require S2 context. Catastrophic stops ≥ 2×Nⁱ. Trade only with the break direction; if TICK is disorderly (±1000), wait for the second push rather than the first spike.

Action items. (1) Bracket logic: parent STOP at rail, OCO STOP = 2×Nⁱ, staged add STOPs at E ± 0.5×Nⁱ. (2) Kill switch after two −1R attempts in a session. (3) Shift to Shell overlays when regime = Balanced or when holding overnight risk intentionally.

Research note: separating playbooks reduces cognitive switching costs and preserves expectancy; pyramids are earned only after realized progress; flat by EOD keeps trailing drawdowns stable.

7) Turtle Shell Option Layer — Hedge on Balanced Days, Hyper‑Growth on Trend Days

Purpose. The Shell exists to protect the futures book on non‑trending days and to hyper‑extend winners on trending days. In ranges, we sell premium for credit so theta offsets the small −1R futures scratches. In trends, we buy convexity (long OTM calls/puts or debit structures) keyed to the breakout so upside accelerates without capping the first futures unit.

Underlying choices. Use SPX/NDX (cash‑settled, broad‑based), or highly liquid ETFs such as SPY/QQQ/IWM and sector ETFs (XLK/XLF/XLE/XLY) when you want finer granularity or correlation tilts. For /NQ exposure via options, prefer NDX or QQQ; for /ES, prefer SPX or SPY. Choose by liquidity, slippage, and margin efficiency.

Technical gating (when to deploy each). Balanced: price inside H20/L20, ADX flat, Nⁱ ≤ 40th pct, IV Rank (IVR) 20–60 → favor credit spreads/condors/flies around rails. Trending: 5m close beyond rail with ADX rising, IVR 10–40 → debit verticals, calendars/diagonals, or long OTM options. Stressed: Nⁱ ≥ 90th pct, VIX z ≥ +1.5 → collars, put spreads, backspreads. Switch automatically when regime flips.

Credit objective in ranges. Target total daily option credit ≈ 0.25–0.50×UnitRisk per futures unit at risk (e.g., if UnitRisk = $500, aim for $125–$250/day net credit across one or two structures). Cap maximum defined option risk ≤ 1.0×UnitRisk so the hedge never dominates heat.

Credit offset math (per session). If UnitRisk = $500 and you take two −1R futures attempts in a Balanced day, the shell aims to harvest ≈ $250–$500 in net credit so the session P&L skews toward flat to small‑positive while preserving readiness for the next breakout.

Management protocol. Enter with defined‑risk structures only; size option risk ≤ futures heat allowance. Standard exits: (1) take 40–60% credit; (2) rail breach → close/flip to trend overlay; (3) short‑strike Delta ≥ 0.35 or price within 0.25×Nᵈ of short strike → roll/flatten that side; (4) flat by EOD unless the shell is the intended overnight carrier.

Technical trader enhancements. Place short strikes where Donchian rails and visible supply/demand zones converge; bias condor width by ADX slope; choose calendars when HV>IV and a squeeze is forming; prefer BWBs when skew is elevated so the broken wing reduces debit while keeping convexity toward the breakout side.

Action items — Shell
  1. Morning: tag regime; compute Nᵈ; record IV Rank for SPX/NDX/SPY/QQQ; pre‑define rail distances in Nᵈ for strike placement.
  2. Balanced: deploy 1–2 defined‑risk credit structures sized so total at‑risk ≤ 1.0×UnitRisk; target 40–60% credit; auto‑close on rail breach.
  3. Trending: on breakout confirmation, add a debit structure (Δ 25–40) with cost ≤ 0.5×UnitRisk per futures unit; roll on Add #2 or IV crush.
  4. Stressed: attach collars/put spreads sized at 5–10% of open trend P&L; remove when Nⁱ and VIX normalize.
  5. Logging: store Greeks (Δ, Γ, Θ), IVR, distance‑to‑rail (in Nᵈ), and rule‑based exits; attribute shell P&L by regime.

8) Risk Model, Position Heat & Kill‑Switches

Purpose & context. The risk engine is the shell’s spine: it converts volatile, path‑dependent prices into constant‑risk bets expressed in N. Its job is simple and ruthless — cap the left tail so the right tail can pay. Position risk lives at three layers: trade (2×Nⁱ stops), session (two‑strike rule; daily stop 1.5–2.0R), and portfolio (heat cap ≤ 4–6% with correlated‑sleeve checks). This section formalizes the math, then explains each factor and the behavioral traps it protects you from.

Preface to formulas (why this framing). Markets change noise faster than they change structure. Expressing entries, stops, adds, and size in N lets you hold policy constant while microstructure and volatility breathe. Standardization makes outcomes comparable across regimes and products, enabling disciplined pyramids and reliable drawdown control.

Core formulas (mechanics first)

Risk unit (daily):            Nᵈ = ATR(20, daily, Wilder)
Risk unit (intraday):         Nⁱ = ATR(20, 5‑minute, Wilder)
$ per 1N / contract:          $1N = Nⁱ × PV × Mult      # ES PV×Mult = $50/pt; MES = $5/pt
Unit risk (dollars):          UnitRisk$ = Equity × risk%
Contracts per unit:           C = floor( UnitRisk$ / $1N )
Initial stop distance:        Stop = 2 × Nⁱ (from entry; long subtract, short add)
Add spacing:                  Ladder = entry ± {0.5, 1.0, 1.5} × Nⁱ
Portfolio heat (cap):         Heat% = Σ(UnitRisk$)/Equity  ≤ 4–6%
Daily risk stop:              Stop_day = 1.5–2.0R (flatten + disable adds)
Drawdown throttles:           −3R (halve unit risk), −5R (micros only), −7R (flat)
Slippage budget (planning):   0.05–0.10 × Nⁱ per touch (enter/exit/add)
Volatility guardrails:        Nⁱ ∈ [ max(Nⁱ, 0.3×Nᵈ), 1.5×median₍20₎(Nⁱ) ]

Factor explanations (what each knob controls)

Nᵈ vs Nⁱ. Use Nᵈ to define the playground (rails, option winging) and Nⁱ to size and protect what you actually trade (5–30m execution). Separating intent from protection allows you to respect daily structure without letting intraday noise overwhelm stops.

$1N and contracts C. Translating N into dollars ensures the same R‑risk today as yesterday. When $1N per ES exceeds UnitRisk$, drop to MES. This preserves add logic and exit math instead of silently widening stops or shrinking R.

Heat. Summing UnitRisk across open units/sleeves caps correlated exposure. A 4–6% range balances compounding with survivability; the shell’s risk counts toward heat only while option gamma/theta materially affects P&L intra‑session.

Stops and adds. Fixed 2×Nⁱ catastrophic stops and 0.5×Nⁱ spacing are empirically robust; they keep you in winners long enough to pyramid while ejecting you quickly from fakes. Do not trail discretionarily — exits are L10ⁱ/H10ⁱ (S1) or L20ⁱ/H20ⁱ (S2).

Guardrails. The volatility floor (0.3×Nᵈ) prevents unrealistically tight stops in sleepy sessions; the ceiling (1.5× median Nⁱ) avoids ballooning unit sizes on panic prints. Slippage budgeting keeps expectancy realistic in fast tape.

Theoretical outcomes (what the distribution looks like)

Let hit rate h=38%, average win W=2.2R, average loss L=1.0R. Expectancy per trade is E=h×W − (1−h)×L = 0.38×2.2 − 0.62×1.0 = +0.36R. Over 300 trades, cost‑adjusted, the median path compounds modestly while the right‑tail (trend clusters) drives most of the total return. Heat caps and daily stops constrain the left‑tail so losing streaks (−3R to −6R) do not cascade into ruin. The shell improves the balanced‑regime slope (credit income) and the trend‑regime convexity (debit verticals/diagonals) without diluting first‑unit futures.

Emotional barriers (and how the system neutralizes them)

Widening stops. Loss aversion tempts late stop moves. Countermeasure: pre‑staged OCO at 2×Nⁱ, broker‑server‑side; no cancel/replace allowed except for volatility guardrails.

Adding early. FOMO invites adds before +0.5×Nⁱ. Countermeasure: stage adds as STOPs at ladder prices; disable market orders for adds.

Revenge trades. Two quick losses trigger over‑trading. Countermeasure: two‑strike rule + 60–90 min cooldown; hide P&L during active management.

Narrative drift. News headlines hijack plan. Countermeasure: only rails trigger. Internals are context filters, not signals; if conflicted, default to Balanced and stand down.

Operational defenses
  • Kill‑switch: One command cancels staged adds, tightens all stops to market, and flattens if daily stop is breached.
  • Throttle ladder: −3R (halve risk), −5R (micros only), −7R (flat) — auto‑enforced by the daemon.
  • Heat police: reject new units when projected heat > cap; allow only exits and hedges.
  • Audit trail: every order carries N, rail, risk%, and regime tags; violations are surfaced on the dashboard.
